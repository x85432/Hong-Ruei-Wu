  3. Learning Journey

    English vs. Math: Never the Twain Shall Meet

    Born in March 2004 and raised in Wufeng District, Taichung.

    Elementary School

    I attended Wufu Elementary School right next to my home — just one class of 17 students — and I never ranked first in my class before third grade.

    Then I set my heart on becoming an engineer, and after scoring only 48 on an English midterm, I asked my mom if I could take English cram classes. English went on to become, without question, my best subject throughout school.

    Junior & Senior High School

    In junior high, I found that math didn't come as naturally to me, so I hoped to repeat my English success by taking math cram classes too.
    Unfortunately, things didn't go as planned. Realizing that math is a prerequisite for becoming an engineer made me very anxious, and nothing had improved by the time the Advanced Subjects Test (similar to the old Department Required Test) was over
    (GSAT math: top 25%; Advanced Subjects Test math: average).

    University

    So I resolutely enrolled in NYCU's Department of Applied Mathematics, hoping to shore up my only weak spot.
    After some twists and turns, I transferred into NYCU's Department of Computer Science in my third year while double majoring in Applied Mathematics, hoping to make my mark in the future.

  4. Learning Journey

    Programming

    The Enlightenment

    In elementary and junior high, Minecraft was the hottest thing around, and I was hooked too.

    Wanting to become a true creator from the ground up, Java became my coding starting line.
    Although back then most of my code was just public class static void, System.out.println()...

    The Renaissance

    After the junior high Comprehensive Assessment Program exam, Python was all the rage, and I realized coding might actually pay well. I started learning Python on YouTube — I still remember that playlist, and how the instructor replied to my comment.

    The Baroque Period

    In high school I discovered programming competitions, entered one using Python and won an honorable mention. In the training program that followed, my teacher had me learn C/C++ — an annoying language where you have to declare everything explicitly.
    Yet I started finding YouTube videos to learn from on my own, and tested my skills on Online Judges. Intro to Computer Science and the department transfer exam in university pushed my Python and C++ skills to a whole new level.

  5. Looking Ahead

    Spreading My Wings

    • Never forget where I started: ever since my elementary-school dream of becoming an engineer, I've been paving a solid road for myself.
    • Embrace change: while remembering where we started, we shouldn't limit ourselves to one particular path — believe that you can do well whichever way you go.
    • Keep moving forward: when facing strangers and unfamiliar fields, opportunities come as long as you're willing to speak up. Enlarging and seizing opportunities is, I think, the ability I need most.
